01

It begins with a number

Every map of "digital talent" starts with one. Ours is 194,482 working professionals - drawn from 213,732 profiles, reaching into 160 countries.

But a headcount is the dullest thing about them. What matters is what they do - and where each kind of talent gathers.

02

What the pool is made of

Two forces lead the pool: engineering & technology (26%) and leadership & founders (26%), with data & AI close behind (16%) - three of the twenty-two job families we track in depth.

This is our baseline - the shape everything else is measured against.

03

The one question we ask

Not "where are the most people" - but "what is each place unusually good at?" For every country we take the slice of its talent in a job and hold it up against the world's slice.

Finance is about 3% of talent worldwide. In Hong Kong it's closer to 14% - five times the concentration. That gap is a signature, and it's what the rest of this story is built on.

04

Where the young are

Now, young talent - measured as a share of each region, never a raw count. It clusters heavily in a few places.

North Africa stands out: 39% of its professionals are early-career, over 4× the world average. Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa also skew young; the West and Latin America are the most senior.

05

They all do the same work

Wherever the young are, they do the same handful of jobs. Software engineering comes first everywhere - a quarter to a half of each region's early-career talent - followed by data and AI.

The mix barely changes from one region to the next.

06

One shared language

Zoom from families to exact titles and it's starker still: software engineer, data scientist, data & AI engineer top the young list in every region.

Careers begin in one language, and only branch into local specialisms as people climb.

In closing

There isn't one map of talent. There are many.

Look long enough and the flat idea of "digital talent" dissolves into something richer. Western Europe advises and consults; Asia leads, manages and hires; and across Africa, Latin America and the Gulf, a younger world leans into data and into building things from scratch. No country is the centre - each is simply the most itself.

Zoom in and those leanings sharpen into signatures. Finance pools in Hong Kong and Luxembourg. Engineering runs deep in Romania, Israel and Ukraine. And founders gather where you would least expect them - Lagos, Jakarta, Istanbul.

The youngest talent writes the clearest line of all. It gathers most densely in North Africa and Asia, and wherever it lands it speaks the same first language - software, then data - before picking up the accent of its region.

None of it comes from counting heads. It comes from asking a better question of every place on the map: not how many, but what are you for.
